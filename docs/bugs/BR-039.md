- **Found:** 2026-11-09
- **Where:** `src/genokeeper/pipeline.py` `pgsc_step`, `src/genokeeper/prs/pgsc.py` `write_target_vcf`
  and `nextflow_config`

`write_target_vcf` wrote one VCF row per position, with every allele the scoring files name at it. The
research run has 27 files, many of them with inferred other alleles, so the alleles pile up on one row.

What the user saw: the run stopped advancing for 7 h 45 min and said nothing, until `Nextflow` was
stopped by hand. Evidence, from the task's `.command.err` and `.nextflow.log` (read by the owner):

- `Error: VCF file has a variant with 354 ALT alleles; this build of plink2 is limited to 254.`, exit 63
  in `PLINK2_VCF`, two seconds after the start, after 9,162,371 variants scanned.
- `FORMAT_SCOREFILES` then ended with 137 at 16 GB and was queued for retry, status `NEW`. It never ran:
  the same line every five minutes until the process was stopped. The executor had 48 GB and 14 CPUs,
  so the task was not short of resources.

Counted on the public scoring files alone (no genotype): 2,860,082 of the 8,030,793 positions carry
more than two alleles across the 27 files, and 33-47% of each score's rows lie on such a position.
`--max-alleles 2` drops those rows, which would have left every research score under the 0.75 overlap
even had the row limit not been hit. The chosen run does not hit this: its 45 files passed.

**What this evidence does not establish:** why the retried task was never submitted. A toy workflow
with a failing task and an out-of-memory one, on the same Nextflow, did not stall, with `finish` or with
`terminate`, so the stall's trigger is not isolated. The counts are by alleles per position, not by
position and REF, so they somewhat overstate how many rows go multi-allelic. Whether pgsc_calc scores
the one-row-per-ALT target through the panel without its `Missing variants` failure, and whether 32 GB
is enough for `FORMAT_SCOREFILES`, can only be seen on the real run. A score row with several inferred
other alleles (3,847,046 of the 35,789,571 rows) can match several target rows, and pgscatalog-match
keeps the first with no fallback: the row is lost if that one is not in the panel, and a no-call if the
person carries one of the others. Only the run's overlaps can show how much that costs.

## Fix

The research run's `pgsc_step` passes `one_row_per_alt=True`, so a position's alleles go on rows of their
own; the chosen run's target keeps its bytes and its cached step. `nextflow_config` carries pgsc_calc's
retry rule with `terminate` for `finish`, set by a `withName` selector because `MATCH_VARIANTS` and
`PLINK2_SCORE` set `finish` themselves and a process's own setting outranks a plain `process` one (a toy
workflow: 30 s with the plain form, 1 s with the selector). It stops the run at the first task that fails
for good, and those two now retry on 137 too. In the research target a heterozygote of two ALTs (`1/2`)
reads `./.` on each one's row (`_prs_sites`): `0/1` would claim a copy of REF the person does not have. The
other writers keep that `0/1` (BR-040): changing them moves the chosen run's and PharmCAT's cached steps.
Covered by `tests/test_pipeline_invariants.py::test_only_the_research_run_writes_one_row_per_alt`,
`tests/test_pgsc.py::test_config_keeps_arm_options_and_adds_network_none`, and
`tests/test_pgsc.py::test_write_target_vcf_one_row_per_alt` for the rows themselves. Deliberately not
changed: `FORMAT_SCOREFILES`' memory, which pgsc_calc's own retry raises to 32 GB once no other failure
ends the run first.
